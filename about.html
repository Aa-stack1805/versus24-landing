About

Built by one person who trains across modalities.

Why Versus exists.

I lift in the morning. I roll in the evening. Two days a week I run easy. Some weeks I have a fight on the schedule and the cut starts a month out. Every other training app made me pick which one of those I cared about most.

Strong logs my sets. Strava logs my runs. Whoop reads my recovery. None of them talk to each other. None of them know that yesterday I sparred six hard rounds and today my squat is going to feel terrible — and that's fine, it's the camp.

Versus is the app I wanted to use. Combat, strength, endurance, sport, mobility — all in one logger. Readiness pulled from HealthKit, distilled into a number I can act on every morning. A coach that knows my actual training, not a generic plan.

The hybrid-athlete frustration.

The fitness app market is bifurcated. Strength apps for lifters. Endurance apps for runners. Recovery apps that bolt on top. Nobody serves the athlete whose week looks like: lift Monday, BJJ Tuesday, run Wednesday, lift Thursday, BJJ Friday, recover Saturday, race Sunday.

That athlete is a real demographic — fighters cross-train strength + conditioning, lifters add BJJ, runners add lifting to stop getting injured. We're underserved on purpose: it's easier to build for one modality and call it a day.

Why solo.

No co-founder. No team. No VC. Just me, an iPhone, a copy of Xcode, and an opinion about what athletes actually need. The whole iOS app, the watchOS app, the backend, the marketing site, the privacy policy — every line shipped by one person.

This isn't a brag. It's a contract: if you tell me something's broken, I fix it that week. If you ask for a feature, you talk to the person who decides whether to build it. There's no support team to escalate to — it's just my email.

"I built this for myself. The fact that other athletes use it is the best part of my day."
