You came here because

Most apps only count one kind of training.

  • You lifted Monday, sparred Tuesday, ran Thursday. Your lifting app saw one session that week and called it a light week.
  • So you keep two apps and a Notes file, and none of the three knows what the other two cost you.
  • Then something starts hurting and you cannot say whether you ramped too fast, because nothing ever added it all up.

How to read the number

What readiness and ACWR are, and what they aren't.

  • Readiness is an estimate. It comes from HRV, sleep, resting heart rate and recent load in Apple Health, compared against your own 30-day baseline. A guide for how to train today, not a verdict on your body.
  • ACWR counts every modality. The acute:chronic workload ratio compares recent work to that baseline. On most apps it only sees one sport, which is why a heavy lifting week can hide inside a running app. Here the roll, the run and the squat session land in the same total.
  • Neither one diagnoses an injury or predicts one. Versus is a training log and a planning tool, not a medical device. The ratio tells you the ramp is steep; it does not tell you that you are hurt, and it is no substitute for noticing that you are.

The coach

It asks before it prescribes.

Sensei runs on Anthropic's Claude, with your last 30 sessions, your current camp, your readiness and your PRs in front of it. That is the whole trick: it answers about your training instead of about training. Ask it for an exercise swap and it wants to know why first. Tell it what happened in a round and it tags the positions and the finishes.
